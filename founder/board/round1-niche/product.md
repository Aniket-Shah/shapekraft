# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There is a one-sentence product hiding in the brief.** "Launch + Automate" (a fast custom site plus one automation, live in 3 weeks, 90+ mobile Lighthouse score, enquiries delivered to inbox or WhatsApp, or it is fixed for free) is concrete and easy to check. It describes what the customer gets, not what the studio does.
- **The way it is built supports quality.** Custom Next.js work assembled from an internal component library is how a small studio keeps its standards consistent across projects, including the parts no client looks at.
- **It has shipped a real B2B site.** A live chemical-trading site with RFQ forms proves the hardest flow on the menu has been delivered at least once.
- **Speed is measurable.** A speed score is a quality claim that a buyer can verify for themselves, and the outreach idea of opening with the prospect's own score builds on that.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** There are six packages, two markets with two currencies and two domains, AI workflows, two care tiers, a paid audit, partnerships, IndiaMART and referrals. All of it rests on about 15–20 hours a week. The Product lens reads this as a menu, not a product. Every extra item is another place where delivery gets stretched thin.
2. **The experience is invisible.** There are no published case studies, the Featured Work section is switched off, the founder cannot appear publicly, and a search for the name returns a different company. In its first five minutes a buyer finds nothing to look at, so the craft cannot show.
3. **Gaps at every hand-off.** Leads arrive by email, the quote builder, WhatsApp and the founder's network. The work is then delivered remotely by one part-time person against a 3-week guarantee. Whatever falls through at those hand-offs is what the customer experiences. Real hours per project are unknown, so the guarantee may be impossible to keep.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could sell only one thing, in one market, until February 2027, what would it be?
- What happens in the customer's first five minutes, from the first message to seeing a draft?
- How many hours did the chemical-trading site really take? Can a 3-week guarantee survive a 15-hour week?
- Which market is cut, and why? India and US buyers differ on price, channel and what they expect.
- Who answers a WhatsApp enquiry at 2pm on a workday, and how fast?
- What is the "one automation" by default? Name it, rather than "e.g."

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut to one offer in one market.** Launch + Automate, with a single named default automation (e.g. enquiry → WhatsApp + auto-reply). Everything else comes off the public site until that offer has paying repeat buyers.
2. **Make the work visible before outreach.** Publish the B2B site as a case study (with the client's permission), with before and after speed scores and the enquiry flow. Turn Featured Work back on.
3. **One lead intake, written down.** Every channel feeds a single queue with a stated response time, plus a written 3-week delivery checklist.
4. **Measure real hours on the next project** and confirm the guarantee holds before promising it at scale.
