# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One offer has a real dream outcome.** The RFQ Launch Kit promises "never lose an order to a faster quote." The only two simulated buyers had lost an order exactly that way. A page-count website can't promise anything that sharp.
- **The buyer is easy to find.** Established distributors list themselves on IndiaMART, and their top public complaint (fake or shared leads) is a pain the studio can talk about.
- **The Quote Desk Pilot is the right risk-reverser.** 12 of 18 non-buyers said a trial on their own price list would change their answer. ₹5,000 for 14 days, credited against the full price, lowers the effort and risk the buyer has to take on.
- **Prices are no longer the cheapest in the market.** The move off $110/$350/$550 ends the race to the bottom on price.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Buyers have no reason to believe it works.** There is one B2B client, no published case study, the Featured Work section is switched off, and a search for the name returns a different company. Trust was the top pass reason (7 of 18), and 14 of 18 asked for proof. Without proof a stronger offer still won't sell.
2. **Most of the catalogue is a commodity.** Starter, Growth and Studio are sold by page count, priced inside the freelancer range, against site builders at $10–$39 a month. Nothing in those tiers makes a buyer feel foolish for saying no. Auto-quote technology is also not a gap, because free and cheap tools already do it.
3. **The market is split and the effort is spread thin.** Six product lines across two countries, run by one person for 15–20 hours a week, with a February 2027 deadline. Micro and small firms bought 0 of 16. The segment that buys is narrow, and the effort isn't aimed at it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Can the chemical-trading client give you one number, such as quote turnaround or enquiries won, that you can publish?
- What guarantee can you afford on the Quote Desk, and what does it cost you if it gets claimed?
- How many hours does a Launch Kit really take? Nobody has this number yet.
- How many established distributors (20–50 staff) can you reach, and what does it cost to get one as a client? This is unknown.
- How does the quote desk handle prices that change daily or differ by customer? Buyers raised this often.
- Does "1 million" mean ₹ or $? It changes which market can get you there.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Lead with one offer: Quote Desk Pilot leading into the RFQ Launch Kit, aimed at established distributors only. Keep the site tiers as background or referral work.
2. Publish one case study with a measured result before scaling outreach.
3. Add a named guarantee (for example, the pilot fee is refunded if quotes aren't faster on their own price list) and the ownership/handover clause.
4. Re-test the price against the distributor band (₹35,000 seen as a bargain, ₹90,000 as expensive) once there is proof, not before.
