# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The craft is real. ShapeKraft builds custom Next.js/React/TypeScript sites with no templates, has an internal component library, and runs a live instant quote builder. That level of care in the parts nobody sees is what the Product lens rewards.
- There is a product inside the studio. The Quote Desk is the one offer built around a customer moment: a buyer asks for a price, and a drafted quote is waiting for approval on WhatsApp. It is also the only offer the simulated buyers asked to try (12 of 18 said a pilot on their own price list would change their answer).
- One B2B client with RFQ forms is already live, so the experience can be shown, not just described.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** The menu has three site tiers in two currencies, the RFQ kit, a pilot, automation, two care plans, demos for watches, perfume and cafés, and dropped products under the same name. A part-time founder with 15–20 hours a week cannot do all of that brilliantly. Right now the business cannot describe itself in one sentence, so a buyer cannot either.
2. **The first five minutes break trust.** A prospect who searches the name finds "ShapeCraft". The studio site has Featured Work switched off, has no case studies, and shows no founder. For buyers whose main objection was trusting a small remote studio (7 of 18), the experience fails before any product is used.
3. **The core moment may fail in use.** Prices that change daily or per customer were raised often. If the Quote Desk drafts a wrong price even once, the trader's trust is gone, and right now there is no evidence that it handles this.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what does ShapeKraft do, and for whom?
- Describe the first five minutes for a distributor: they get the outreach, then what? Where are the seams (email, quote builder, WhatsApp, call) and who owns each one?
- How does the Quote Desk handle a price that changed this morning, or a price that only one customer gets?
- What will you stop selling on Monday?
- How many hours does one site really take, and how many clients can you serve well at 15–20 hours a week? (Unknown today.)
- Can the chemical-trading client be published as a case study?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one lead offer and one buyer (the evidence points to established distributors and the Quote Desk), and write it as one sentence on the homepage. Hide or archive the vertical demos and anything that has nothing to do with that offer.
2. Fix the first touchpoints before doing more outreach: publish one case study from the live B2B client, turn Featured Work back on, and fix the problem that searches for the name return a different company.
3. Run paid Quote Desk pilots on real price lists, including price changes. Track how many draft quotes are approved without edits. That number is open today.
4. Map every handover from first contact to the care plan, and set a capacity limit so quality does not slip.
