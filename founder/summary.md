# Summary

**ShapeKraft** is a remote studio that builds custom, fast websites and wires AI automation and integrations into them, for small and growing businesses and B2B teams in India and the US/global market.

**Verdict: Profitable, on estimated numbers.** Read this as "the arithmetic works", not "it will sell":

- **Margin:** each project leaves $675 (91%) after its own costs; at 6 projects a month the business keeps 49% after the estimated founder draw and overheads.
- **Break-even:** 3 projects a month, against an assumed capacity of 8.
- **Year 1:** $9,900 operating profit on $35,520 of revenue, about $3,725 of cash needed before it pays for itself, startup spend earned back in month 8.
- **What is not backed by evidence:** the volume (6 projects a month by month 9), the $740 average price, the founder draw and the capacity are all my assumptions. At half the planned volume, year 1 is a $6,300 loss. The buyer panel was **not run**, so no simulated buyers have tested the offer or the price.

**Where the board agreed and disagreed.** Two lenses said fund if, one said pass (average 5.7 out of 10). All agreed the **price tells the wrong story**: $110 to $550 in the US sits below the public freelancer range of roughly $500 to $5,000, which undercuts a "no templates, pure craft" brand. They also agreed there is **no proof yet** (3+ projects, no case studies). They disagreed on whether a custom-website studio has any lasting advantage; the pass came from the Monopoly lens.

**Biggest risk:** demand, not cost. Pipeline volume decides everything: break-even is 3 projects a month and the business loses money below that. The fix is a narrower niche (B2B teams that need a site plus automation) and proof (2 to 3 measured case studies).

**What the founder needs to start:** about $3,725 of cash on the assumed ramp, or $800 of startup spend if cash is tight and the first projects come quickly.

**The one thing to do this week:** test a higher US price. Put a $1,500 Growth tier on the quote page alongside five real conversations ("what did you expect to pay?"). At $1,500 the CFO shows break-even falling to 2 projects a month and year 1 profit rising to $46,380 at the same volume, which is an upper bound.

_The panel is simulated buyers (not run here) and the numbers are projections from the inputs; real customers and real quotes confirm them. Not financial, legal or tax advice._
