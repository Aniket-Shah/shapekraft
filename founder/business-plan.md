## The idea

- **What it is:** a global, remote digital engineering studio (founded 2025) that builds custom websites (Next.js/React/TypeScript, no templates) plus AI automation, performance optimisation and webhook/integration work.
- **Who it is for:** small and growing businesses and startups that want a custom site rather than a template, B2B companies that need proposals, decks and ongoing maintenance (AMC), and teams that want AI or integrations wired into the tools they already use.
- **What it sells, at what price:** packages Basic $110 / Pro $350 / Advanced $550 (₹8,000 / ₹25,000 / ₹40,000), plus $30 (₹2,000) per page, add-ons $150–$350, and custom-priced B2B. Payment split 40% design / 30% build / 30% launch. Source: [BUSINESS.md](../BUSINESS.md).
- **Where and how:** online, two markets (India via shapekraft.in in INR, US/global via shapekraft.co in USD). Leads arrive by email, the quote builder and WhatsApp.
- **Budget and constraints:** not stated. Team size, founder pay, capacity and revenue goals are TBD in BUSINESS.md, so the numbers in `numbers.json` are estimates the founder must correct.

## Summary

**ShapeKraft** is a remote studio that builds custom, fast websites and wires AI automation and integrations into them, for small and growing businesses and B2B teams in India and the US/global market.

**Verdict: Profitable, on estimated numbers.** Read this as "the arithmetic works", not "it will sell":

- **Margin:** each project leaves $675 (91%) after its own costs; at 6 projects a month the business keeps 49% after the estimated founder draw and overheads.
- **Break-even:** 3 projects a month, against an assumed capacity of 8.
- **Year 1:** $9,900 operating profit on $35,520 of revenue, about $3,725 of cash needed before it pays for itself, startup spend earned back in month 8.
- **What is not backed by evidence:** the volume (6 projects a month by month 9), the $740 average price, the founder draw and the capacity are all my assumptions. At half the planned volume, year 1 is a $6,300 loss. The buyer panel was **not run**, so no simulated buyers have tested the offer or the price.

**Where the board agreed and disagreed.** Two lenses said fund if, one said pass (average 5.7 out of 10). All agreed the **price tells the wrong story**: $110 to $550 in the US sits below the public freelancer range of roughly $500 to $5,000, which undercuts a "no templates, pure craft" brand. They also agreed there is **no proof yet** (3+ projects, no case studies). They disagreed on whether a custom-website studio has any lasting advantage; the pass came from the Monopoly lens.

**Biggest risk:** demand, not cost. Pipeline volume decides everything: break-even is 3 projects a month and the business loses money below that. The fix is a narrower niche (B2B teams that need a site plus automation) and proof (2 to 3 measured case studies).

**What the founder needs to start:** about $3,725 of cash on the assumed ramp, or $800 of startup spend if cash is tight and the first projects come quickly.

**The one thing to do this week:** test a higher US price. Put a $1,500 Growth tier on the quote page alongside five real conversations ("what did you expect to pay?"). At $1,500 the CFO shows break-even falling to 2 projects a month and year 1 profit rising to $46,380 at the same volume, which is an upper bound.

### Risks more than one lens raised (go first)

1. **The price tells the wrong story.** Packages start at $110 and top out at $550 in USD, while public sources put US freelancer small-business sites at roughly $500 to $5,000 and agencies far higher ([competitors.md](competitors.md)). A "no templates, pure craft" studio priced below the cheapest freelancer reads as a template shop, and it leaves no room to deliver the engineering the brand promises. Raised by the Offers and Product lenses.
2. **No proof yet.** The site shows "3+ projects shipped" and a switched-off Featured Work section. A buyer has nothing to check the promise against. Raised by the Offers and Monopoly lenses.
3. **"Custom websites" is a crowded market with no moat.** Freelancers, agencies and no-code builders (Framer, Webflow, Wix) all compete for the same buyer. Raised by the Monopoly and Offers lenses.

### Where they disagree

- **Monopoly says pass; the other two say fund if.** The Monopoly lens sees no durable advantage in building websites. The Product lens sees real craft in the site itself, and the Offers lens sees an upsell path (AI automation, integrations) that is harder to copy. The business is fundable only if it narrows to where those advantages live.

### The gap (a hypothesis, not yet proven)

On price alone, ShapeKraft's USD packages sit **below the freelancer range**, so it is not occupying a gap, it is undercutting the cheapest option while claiming the most premium positioning. The more promising gap is **engineering plus automation**: freelancers and builders sell a site; few advertise AI workflows and integrations bundled with a fast custom build. That claim needs checking against named competitors' offers before relying on it.

India looks different: the ₹8,000 to ₹40,000 packages sit inside the freelancer range ₹10,000 to ₹80,000, so they are competitive rather than anomalous.

## The numbers

> **Reading note:** this studio sells projects, not daily units, so the tool's "a day" columns here mean **a month** (`days_per_month` is set to 1). Break-even "3 projects a day" = **3 projects a month**.

### The CFO's note

- **The margin:** each project leaves **$675 (91%)** after its own costs. At 6 projects a month the business keeps **49%** of revenue after the estimated founder draw and overheads.
- **The line to watch:** *volume*, not cost. Break-even is 3 projects a month. At half the planned volume (3/month) the margin falls to **7%** and year 1 is a **$6,300 loss**, needing **$7,700** of cash. Price is the other lever (below).
- **Cash:** about **$3,725** before it pays for itself, assuming the slow ramp (1, 1, 2, 3 projects in months 1 to 4). Startup money is earned back in month 8.
- **Three ways to improve the margin** (from the tool's what-ifs at the same volume):
  1. **Raise the average price to $1,500:** margin 49% to 75%, break-even 3 to 2 projects a month, year 1 profit $9,900 to $46,380, cash needed $3,725 to $1,680. This is the biggest lever and fits where freelancers actually price (see `competitors.md`). Demand at that price is unproven.
  2. **Hold volume near plan:** volume -20% alone cuts year 1 profit from $9,900 to $3,420. Pipeline matters more than cutting tool costs (unit costs +15% only costs $468).
  3. **Raise the average price to $2,500** (B2B and Advanced focus): margin 85%, break-even 1 project a month, cash needed $800. Treat as an upper bound.
- **Board conditions about money:** see `board.md`: positive contribution (met), a break-even under capacity (met, 3 vs 8), a tested price (not met yet).
- **Inputs are estimates.** The average price of $740, the founder draw, the volume and the capacity are my assumptions from the price list in BUSINESS.md (see `cfo-sources.md`). Not financial, tax or legal advice; an accountant should check structure and tax before money moves. Revenue is before sales tax or GST.
